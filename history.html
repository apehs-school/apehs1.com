History & Milestones

The merged Ardenne Preparatory and Extension High School came into existence on September 1, 2000. Prior to this date, Ardenne Preparatory School and Ardenne Extension High School operated as two separate institutions under the same Board of Governors. Both schools shared the same campus, with the Preparatory School in session from 7:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. daily, and the Extension High School from 12:30 p.m. to 5:05 p.m. However, neither school satisfied the Ministry of Education's required contact hours — the number of hours teachers meet with students each day. As a result, the institutions were at a disadvantage when compared with similar schools. To address this issue, it became necessary to streamline administrative functions and expand the co-curricular programme. The merger of both institutions therefore became essential.

In the early years, the two schools were registered as a single entity known as Ardenne Preparatory and Evening Institute. Despite this, they continued to function independently. In 1996, the Ministry of Education officially registered them as separate schools. Today, they stand united as one institution.

📘 The Preparatory School

Ardenne Preparatory School was established in 1974 when Rev. Rupert Lawrence sought and received permission from the then Ardenne High School Board and the Executive Council of the Church to start a private school that would serve as a feeder institution for Ardenne High School. The premises at 37 Hope Road, which previously housed the Jamaica Bible Institute, were identified as the location for the new school.

Rev. Rupert Lawrence served as the first Chairman of the Board. Both Rev. Lawrence and Mrs. Lily Brown dedicated themselves to the establishment of the school, and they secured the services of Mrs. Daphnie Bailey, who was appointed as the first Principal. It was Mrs. Brown who developed the school’s motto — Nil Nisi Optimum Hic Est Agendum, meaning “Nothing but the Best is Done Here.”

The school began with an enrolment of 100 students and was housed in the current Administrative Block, which underwent extensive repairs to make it suitable for the children. The school continued to grow, and by 1993 enrolment had increased to 560 students. This led the Board to reduce enrolment to 400, which was the number that could be comfortably accommodated at the time. The current enrolment stands at 434 students.

Principals who have served the school in the Preparatory Division are:

  • Mrs. Daphnie Ramsay Bailey (1974–1977)
  • Mrs. Olive Wallace (1977–1980)
  • Mrs. Norma Thompson (1980–1986)
  • Mrs. Edna Hutchinson (1986–1989)
  • Mrs. Ishbel Feurtado (1989–1999)
  • Mrs. Jeanette Hall – Acting (1999–2000)
  • Ms. Donna Dorman – Provisional (2000–2001)
  • Mrs. Doret Solomon (2001–2006)
  • Rev. Myrtle Clarke (2006–Present)

🎓 The Extension High School

The Extension High School was also established at 37 Hope Road in September 1980, with Mrs. Lily Brown serving as the founding Principal. As with the Preparatory School, the vision for the Extension School originated with Rev. Rupert Lawrence, who sought to provide a second chance for students of Ardenne High School who had been unsuccessful in the General Certificate Examination.

An advertisement inviting applications was placed in the daily newspaper, and more than 300 applications were received from parents seeking places in the first, second, third, and fifth forms. In September 1981, the Evening Institute, as it was then known, officially opened with an enrolment of 245 students, 32 of whom were fifth-form repeaters. Mrs. Lily Brown was appointed Principal of the institution.

During this period, accommodation was extremely limited. The space intended to serve as the Principal’s office was used instead as a staff room and a Technical Drawing room. The science laboratories and playfield at Ardenne High School were also made available to Evening Institute students after 3:30 p.m. on selected afternoons.

Both the Preparatory School and the Evening Institute grew rapidly, creating an urgent need for additional classrooms. As a result, a building fund was established to support future expansion.

🏫 Campus Development

In 1983, a new building consisting of four classrooms and two sets of bathrooms was constructed. Dr. Noel Brown of JenTech Limited supervised the construction of this facility at no cost to the school. An upper floor was later added to the building, which now houses three classrooms, a guidance and counselling room, staff facilities, and a small science laboratory.

In the 1999–2000 academic year, metal containers were purchased and converted to accommodate a library, computer laboratory, music room, and two additional classrooms. During the 2000–2001 academic year, nine additional classrooms were constructed, and both a parking lot and a playfield were established.

✝ Christian Foundation

Our school was founded on Christian principles and is committed to providing quality education within a Christian environment. As a result, the spiritual development of our students is of utmost importance. This dimension of school life is nurtured through daily devotions, Inter-Schools’ Christian Fellowship (I.S.C.F.), Good News Club, Christian Emphasis Week, special services, and the integration of Christian values into classroom teaching. Through these activities, many students have committed their lives to Christ.
